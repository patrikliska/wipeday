# Proposal D: "Evolution, not revolution"

Wipe Day becomes an incremental game by **converting what is already built, drawn and tested**
instead of replacing it. Feasibility first: maximum reuse, the fewest phases, one clean wipe.
Every name marked *(proposal)* needs the owner's pass. Every number is a starting point for
the simulator, not a promise.

---

## 1. Pitch and pillars

**Pitch.** You wash up on a wrecked island with nothing but your hands. You tap trees and rocks
like crazy, hire hands, then the survivors who run the lines for you. In a single run you climb
Twig → Timber → Stone → Sheet Metal → Armored, and you bolt together a ridiculous homemade
missile, *the Kettle* (proposal): a boiler on stilts, a traffic-cone nose and every charge you
can make. When the run slows down, you lift the upturned jam jar off *the Big Red* (proposal)
and press it. The Kettle wobbles off the pad, arcs out over the sea, hesitates, and comes back.
The island is gone. You earn *Ash* (proposal) and spend it on a tree laid out over the island's
own chart, and the next island goes up faster. Everything a friend sees today is still there,
now running on an exponential engine: the base, the 18 buildings, the nodes, the crew, the
fogged map, the report cards, the feed and the bot.

Pillars:

1. **Tap, then hand it off.** Every new chain arrives manual (tap to chop, tap to crank the
   loom) and turns automatic when a survivor takes it over: the arc repeats every era.
2. **The base tells the story, faster.** The same procedural art, driven by counts: buildings
   redraw at 1, 25 and 100 levels, hands appear at nodes, the Kettle rises on the slope.
3. **Chains, not counters (kept).** 18 run materials over five eras; the advisor names the
   bottleneck.
4. **The island forgets, you don't.** The nuke takes the island; Ash, the tree, scrap,
   blueprints, crew levels and the logbook stay.
5. **One race, different schedules.** Offline counts at 100% inside a growing window; active
   play earns bursts, not a different game. Friends compare launches, never raw amounts.

### 2.3 The first day

- **Active** (on and off for 3-4 hours): 3-5 launches, 60-100 Ash ever, Glow +300-500%,
  15-25 tree nodes.
- **Casual** (three 2-minute check-ins): Timber and Mara in the morning, Stone and the pad at
  lunch, the frame and warhead in the evening, with the 6-hour offline window carrying
  production between them. The first launch comes on day 2's morning. That is fine, because
  the idle progress was real, and both see the same first-launch card.

### 2.4 The first week

Casual players launch 5-8 times, active ones 15-30. A typical day is a launch with breakfast
(the "daily nuke" ritual), a mid-day check that sends expeditions, and a stew served before bed
to bank the night. Sheet Metal becomes routine by runs 4-6 (plates, gears, dock, radio mast).
Doctrines open at launch 3 and the first keystone slot at launch 4. The tree reaches 40-80
nodes in 4-5 clusters; 5-10 blueprints are researched; trips reach tier 3 sites.

### 2.5 The first month

Casual players have launched 25-35 times, active ones 60-100. Armored is a normal end of a run
and the Quartermaster auto-buys hands. The tree holds about 200 of ~360 nodes (the simulator
caps optimal play at 60%). Deep sites (Submarine Pen, Offshore Platform) are 8-12 h trips that
survive launches. Convoys run weekly with monthly titles. The multiplier per launch has fallen
from ×2-3 to about ×1.3, and the horizon list shows "Exodus at 50 launches".

### 2.6 A player returning after 2 days away

The welcome-back card: "Away 2 d 4 h. The yard ran 12 of 12 h (full at 03:10 yesterday).
+1.4B timber, +220M ingots… 3 expeditions came back: +41 scrap, blueprint: Strongbox. Ashfall:
Dax launched twice (+20% for your next hour)." One primary: "Back to work". *Rested crew*
(proposal), today's catch-up guardrail, then gives 10 minutes of ×3 lines. The advisor's next
primary is usually the Big Red ("+31 Ash, ripe"), because a run left for 2 days has plateaued.
Nothing was lost: the window only stopped accrual ("never destroys" survives).

## 3. The run

### 3.1 What the player buys, and why

The **Yard** (proposal), a shop drawer, has four kinds of row:

- **Lines**, the incremental generators: five node lines (Treeline, Rocks, Fibre patch, Ore
  seam, Sulfur vent) where you buy hands by count, and every building as a levelled line with
  no cap and three drawn stages. Producers add output, converters turn input into output,
  boosters multiply, utility lines extend the window, crew slots and flotsam. They are the
  minute-to-minute "one more".
- **Era:** one big card for the next base tier: costly, doubles everything, brings new lines
  and the next tool. The half-hour goal.
- **Crew:** the 12 named survivors, hired one by one each run at a rising cost and assigned as
  foremen. The relief of automation.
- **The Kettle:** four stages, then the button. The end of the run.

**The phone screen.** The scene fills the top ~55%, reframed so the base fills today's empty
sky and node targets are at least 64 px. The Yard drawer sits below: collapsed, the advisor's
primary row plus the next two; expanded, the full list with ×1 / ×10 / ×25 / Max. A round
ability button sits above it in thumb reach, and the nav row keeps five actions (D45): Yard,
Crew, Map, Tree, Feed. Rule 6.3.1 holds: the advisor crowns one row, and other affordable rows
are enabled but secondary.

### 3.2 The resource model

There are **18 run materials**, down from 23: raw timber, stone, fibre, food, ore, sulfur_ore,
hide, fat; refined ingots, sulfur, fuel, leather; parts planks, rope, plates, gears, gunpowder,
charge. Cut: cloth (rope takes its uses), charcoal (the kiln takes timber), frames and springs
(gears take their place). Scrap leaves the run altogether (section 6).

Each era adds 2-5 materials, and the top bar shows only the era's 4-5 plus whatever the advisor
names as the bottleneck, so a phone never shows 18 numbers. Why this many: "chains, not
counters" is the game's identity, and chains give the advisor's pipe puzzle its meaning (Egg,
Inc.'s green, amber and red depot bar). Why not 23: each material is one more curve to balance
and one more number on a phone.

**Haul** (proposal): every unit produced counts towards the run's Haul at its reference value.
The existing `den.json5 refPer100` table moves to `resources.json5` as `value`, rescaled so that
timber = 1. Haul is the one number that spans every material: it sets the nuke yield and feeds
the leaderboards and the bot card.

### 3.3 Formulas, with example numbers

- **Next level:** `cost(n) = base × r^n` for n owned, r 1.07-1.15 per line (cheap lines steep,
  late lines gentle, as in AdVenture Capitalist).
- **Buy k at once:** `base × r^n × (r^k − 1) / (r − 1)`.
- **Buy max:** `floor(log_r(stock × (r − 1) / (base × r^n) + 1))`.
- **Line output per second:** `perLevel × n × 2^m × eraMult × buckets`, where:
  - m is the number of milestones reached;
  - `eraMult = 2^eraIndex`;
  - the buckets multiply in a fixed order: the line's own "inc" (the sum of +% on that line) ×
    the global "inc" × each "more" node × Glow `(1 + 0.05 × Ash ever)` × buffs (Frenzy, Rally,
    meal, Ashfall) × the doctrine.
- **Converters:** `throughput = perLevel × n × buckets` units per second, while there is input
  and a foreman runs the line. Without a foreman, a tap starts one 3 s cycle that yields
  `throughput × 3`. The furnace at level 1 converts 1 ore per second.
- **A tap:** `toolPower × eraMult × tapBuckets + share × (that node's line output per second)`,
  ×5 on the weak spot. Tool power: Rock 1, Stone Tools 3, Iron 8, Salvaged 20, Power 50.

Worked example: treeline hands, base 10 timber, r = 1.12, 0.4 timber/s each, milestones ×2 at
10, 25 and 50 and ×3 at 100 (before era and global multipliers).

| Owned | Next one costs | Total spent | Output | Payback of the next one |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 31 | 176 | 8/s | 39 s |
| 25 | 170 | 1,333 | 40/s | 1.8 min |
| 50 | 2,890 | 24,000 | 160/s | 15 min |
| 100 | 835k | 7.0M | 960/s | 24 h |

The wall around 60-80 hands is intended: eras and other lines take over, and finally the nuke
(exponential cost always beats polynomial output).

**A tap, worked out.** At 40 timber/s with Stone Tools and campfire level 10 (a 20% share), a
tap pays 3 + 8 = 11 timber: 88/s at 8 taps/s, against 40/s passive. By minute 30 passive
income is ten times that, so taps fall to about 20% of income outside bursts. The simulator
asserts the click share: at least 60% in minutes 0-5, 15-35% at minute 30 for a steady
tapper, at most 15% after the first launch outside bursts and the Afterglow.

### 3.4 Ownership milestones

Per line at 10, 25, 50, 100, 150, 200, 300, 400, 500, then every 100: ×2, or ×3 at each
hundred. The drawing switches to stage 2 at 25 and stage 3 at 100 (today's L1/L2/L3 art, so the
54 existing drawings become milestone art), with a count badge. Island milestones (AdCap's
all-business unlocks): every unlocked line at 25 gives all ×2, at 50 all ×2 again, at 100
flotsam twice as often. A thin bar under each row shows the next: "38/50 → ×2".

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Twig | none | treeline, rocks, workbench, campfire; Rock, then Stone Tools | 0:00 |

or builders). Era costs follow one geometric curve, ×8-15 per era in resource-hours, replacing
today's ×3.0 / ×9.6 / ×0.5. Armored's yield bonus is a reason to push a run further.

### 3.6 The click verb

- **Hits.** Tap any standing node for one hit: no 5-hit run, no 4.5 s window, no daily haul
  (D63 retired). Today's marker becomes the weak spot: ×5 (×10 with a tree node), then it jumps.
- **Felling.** After 20 hits (15 for rocks) the node falls or crumbles, pays a 10-hit bonus
  with a 1% scrap chance, and regrows in 8-20 real seconds. The player hops between 7-8 nodes;
  the fall animation is the reward beat.
- **Stations.** Tapping a building with no foreman runs one cycle (the converter verb).
  Buildings become tappable in the scene, which closes an open ui-review item.
- **What keeps clicking relevant:** the tap share and a better tool every era; the Afterglow
  after each launch, taps × `(1 + 4 × 2^(−t / 10 min))` in closed form; Hot Hands flotsam;
  Rally's auto-taps; keystones like *Knuckle Down*. The clicker build exists; the idler never
  needs it.
- **Transport:** batched, capped at 15 taps/s plus a burst of 30 (section 9).

### 3.7 Active bursts (real seconds only, rule 9)

**Flotsam** (barrels, reworked): a crate, a gull or a flare every 3-8 minutes, staying 20 s
(watchtower and Tidal Flats nodes extend that). Rolls: Windfall, min(15 minutes of output, 10%
of stock) per material; Frenzy ×7 for 60 s; Hot Hands, taps ×50 for 15 s; 3-12 scrap; a 2%
blueprint. The server seeds the schedule per player; spawns while offline drift away, as
barrels do today.

**Abilities** (Gather's slot becomes an ability rail):

| Ability | Effect | Cooldown | Unlocked by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rally | lines ×3 plus 5 auto-taps per second, for 30 s | 10 min | Timber era |
| Overtime | converters ×5 for 60 s | 30 min | tree |
| Flare | calls a flotsam now | 1 h | radio mast |
| Second Wind | resets the other cooldowns | 4 h | tree |

Effects multiply: Flare → Frenzy → Rally → Overtime is the active player's 2-minute check-in.

**Meals** (the idle buff, kept from W3): the campfire cooks food into Roast, Stew or Feast.
Serving one gives +50%, ×2 or ×3 for 1, 2 or 4 hours of game time, offline included. This is
the idle player's lever.

| Step | When | By what | Removes the need for |

| Hands | 0:15 | node line counts | tapping for income |
| Foremen | 6 min | hired survivors | tapping converters |
| Starter kits | launch 2 on | milestones, tree | the slow opening |

**Crew** are today's 12 named survivors, hired each run (each hire ×5 dearer). Levels and XP
persist across launches (today's veterans), +10% per level to the line they run. Traits matter
(mule +50% on node lines, tinkerer +50% on converters, cook meals ×1.5, scavenger trips).
Tiredness, sleep and injuries go; bonds stay only on expeditions.

### 3.9 The offline model

- **The Stockpile window** (proposal). Lines and foremen run at 100% offline, up to a window of
  6 h at the start, +10 min per warehouse level, +1/+2/+4 h per Crate, Large Crate or Strongbox
  in the era's box slots (today's 2/4/8/12/16), +2 h per Deep Cellars rank, ×2 with Night
  Shift. Target 24 h by week 2, ceiling 48 h. When the window ends accrual stops and nothing is
  lost: today's storage-cap rule, measured in hours, shown as one window bar.
- **Settling is exact:** piecewise linear (section 9), buy-max in closed form.
- **Online only:** abilities, flotsam, the Afterglow (bursts reward presence). **Offline too:**
  meals and expeditions. Returning players get today's welcome-back modal, one primary.

## 4. The nuke

### 4.1 How it is unlocked: the Kettle

The Signal's code (a staged shared project) becomes the per-player Kettle. The Signal's tower
spot and staged drawing become the rocket on the slope.

| Stage | First-run cost | Drawn as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pad | 30k stone, 3k planks | a ring of pallets with a ladder |
| Frame | 8k ingots, 2k rope | a boiler on stilts |
| Warhead | 300 charges | a taped crate in the nose, a traffic-cone tip |
| Fuel | 3k fuel | jerrycans, a hose, a dripping tank |

Visible but locked from Stone, with its reason. Costs are fixed per run (tree nodes discount

### 4.2 Staging: the UX and the comedic beats

1. **Foreshadowing (Stone era).** The pad goes up with a "DO NOT (seriously)" sign. The Big Red
   sits on a cable-spool table under an upturned jam jar, with its own look (a yellow-and-black
   hazard ring, a dome, a wobble), never the advisor's red primary. Proposal: move the primary
   token from red to signal orange, so red means only the Big Red.
2. **Armed.** The button twitches, gulls land on the Kettle, a survivor paints a face on it.
   A live readout: "+23 Ash now · 9/h · best 12/h".
3. **Commitment (rule 4).** One tap opens a card: the gain, Glow before and after, kept, lost,
   and a joke line ("Wait, I just fixed the roof." Dax). "Lift the lid", then hold 2 s while a
   tone rises. Cancel is always there.
4. **The launch** (6-8 s, skippable from the second time, a fade with reduced motion). The crew
   sprint to the dock and row off. The Kettle sputters, tips, fires, arcs out to sea,
   hesitates, U-turns and whistles back. A white flash (CSS overlay), a fireball on `lights`, a
   mushroom cloud of puff sprites, camera shake; the tint goes burnt orange, then ash grey.
5. **The Afterglow.** A crater under a green sky that clears over 10 minutes, ash flakes (a new
   mode of the weather layer), the crew paddling back wearing pots as helmets. The crater
   glows, and taps pay ×5, fading.
6. **The card** (the season-over card, reused): "Run 7 · 2 h 14 m · Haul 4.1T · +42 Ash · Glow
   +210% → +420%". Primary: "Spend Ash" if a node is affordable, else "Rebuild".
7. **The feed and Discord:** "Patrik pressed the Big Red. Run 7, 2 h 14 m, +42 Ash." Friends get
   Ashfall (section 8).

### 4.3 The yield formula

**The formula:** `AshEver(L) = floor(10 × sqrt(L / L0))`, with L the lifetime Haul over all
runs. A launch gains `AshEver(L + H) − AshEver(L)` for this run's Haul H: Cookie Clicker's
delta model, so launching early again and again cannot farm Ash. L0 is calibrated so the
median first launch yields 12-20 Ash. Doubling your Ash needs 4× the lifetime Haul (AdCap's
square root, generous for a small group).

**What one Ash is worth:** spent, it buys tree nodes; and every Ash ever earned adds +5% to all
output as Glow, which spending never takes away (Cookie Clicker's split, avoiding AdCap's
break-even sums). A 20-Ash first launch doubles output and also buys 3-5 nodes. Modifiers: the
Armored casing ×1.5, tree nodes, Doctrines (+30-50%), Newcomer's luck (×2).

**When to launch:** the button shows the gain per hour now against the run's best. The advisor
crowns Launch when the gain is at least 50% of Ash ever and the rate has fallen below 80% of
the best: the classic optimum, shown rather than left to a spreadsheet.

### 4.7 A second prestige layer (the hook): Exodus

At launch 50 (or 100k Ash ever) the player can sail away from the dock. Today's season reset
(`endSeason`, `carryOver`) becomes this personal second layer: it resets Ash, Glow and the
permanent tree; keeps keystones, crew, blueprints, the logbook and cosmetics; pays *Charts*
(proposal), `floor(5 × log10(AshEver))`, each ×1.25 to one chosen cluster; and reseeds the 13
regions with a sixth era. Designed now, built once the simulator shows a per-launch gain below
×1.3 for a week.

## 5. The tree

### 5.1 Size and structure

About 360 nodes at R4 (120 at the first deploy), with room for 500, laid out over the island's
own chart: Landing is the centre and each of the other 12 existing regions is a cluster of
about 30 nodes in 3-4 rings. The overview is the map's terrain at night (MapView reused), so the
tree reads as "what the island remembers".

| Quarry Hills | stone, ore, rocks |

### 5.2 Node types and costs

| Type | Share | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| small stat | 50% | +x% to one bucket, ranks 1-5 |
| notable | 20% | a named mechanic |
| unlock | 10% | an ability, a fourth flotsam kind, a doctrine |
| automation and QoL | 10% | auto-buyers, kept stages, starting eras |
| keystone | 4% (~14) | bends a rule, at a cost |
| cluster completion | 3% (12) | a bonus for owning a whole cluster |

Never more than three small nodes in a row before a notable or an unlock. Cost by ring: bases
of 1, 5, 25, 120, 600 and 3,000 Ash, ×1.5 per step inside a ring, keystones 10× their ring.
Target: 3-8 nodes per launch (simulator-asserted). About 2k Ash finishes rings 1-3 and about
250k buys everything: months.

### 5.3 Respec and loadout

Permanent nodes are never refunded, except a free full respec after any balance change. Keystones
are owned for good but only work in loadout slots (1 to 4, by launch count and tree nodes),
swapped free at every launch: a different build each run, without regret.

### 5.4 Reading it on a 390 px phone

The overview is the island with 13 cluster badges, each with a progress ring that glows when
something is affordable. A tap opens one cluster screen: at most 35 nodes, each at least 44 px
(D48), labels in screen space (D46) only at that zoom. Tapping a far node highlights the path
and its total cost, with one Buy. A bottom sheet shows the icon, effect now → next, the cost,
and Buy (the one primary) or "Need 12 more Ash". Filter chips: Affordable, Taps, Crew, Offline,
Automation. The advisor glows one node. Purchases are predicted instantly (section 9).

fragments" idea is served by Rust's find-or-pay rule without the Rust item name: a blueprint
found on a trip is free to learn, researching one costs scrap.

**Names:** Ash, Glow, Haul and Charts (all proposals). "Fallout", "Rads", "Vault" and "Blueprint
fragments" are deliberately avoided (D43).

## 8. Social layer (2-10 friends, far apart)

Every comparison is a count, a time or a ratio, never an absolute amount.

- **Launch news** in the feed and `#wipe-day-idle` (existing pipeline, new `launched` event).
- **Ashfall** (proposal): a friend's launch gives everyone else +10% for 1 h, stacking to +50%,
  kept for an absent player's first hour back.
- **Care package:** once a day, send a friend 30 minutes of their own production; you get 5
  scrap.
- **Convoys** (contracts reworked, Egg, Inc.'s model without the pressure): weekly, 72 h, three
  goals counted in shares; a share is 20 minutes of the giver's own production of a named
  material; goals scale with member count. Rewards: scrap and a *Pennant* (proposal), a
  permanent ×1.02 to all output, compounding, at most 4 a month. Past convoys reopen solo at
  half reward for late joiners.
- **Leaderboards**, weekly with monthly titles, in the six existing slots: Biggest Blast (Ash
  gained over Ash ever), Fastest Launch (run start to a launch worth 50% of Ash ever), Most
  Launches, Tree (nodes), Pathfinder (sites), Lucky Hand (flotsam), plus convoy shares.
- **Catch-up:** *Newcomer's luck* (proposal), Ash ×2 until your Ash ever reaches 25% of the
  group's median; flotsam windfalls scaled up to ×5 far behind the expected curve and down to
  ×0.5 far ahead (Egg, Inc.'s drone factor).
- **Visit** (an R5 stretch): tap a friend's name to see their island, read-only.

## 9. Architecture, and an honest reuse estimate

**Numbers.** Plain doubles for materials, the Haul and Ash; integers for counts. This retires
"integers in state" for amounts (a new decision); a `Qty` alias keeps a later Decimal switch a
type change. A `Number.isFinite` guard in the domain and in `save()`, zod
`z.number().finite().nonnegative()`, and `abbrev` extended with T, Qa, Qi, Sx, Sp, Oc, No, Dc,
then `1.23e36` (three significant digits, shared with the bot). Magnitude budget: run 1 ~1e6
Haul, week 1 ~1e12, month 1 ~1e20, month 3 ~1e45; Exodus brings it back, far below 1e308.

**Settling.** `settleAll` becomes one integrator walking forward in time. Lines are piecewise
linear with breakpoints at the window's end, a converter's input running dry, auto-buys and
buff ends; the Afterglow is closed-form. A step budget of 2,000, buy-max in closed form,
fractional remainders kept as doubles, and a property test
`settle(settle(s, t1), t2) ≈ settle(s, t2)` within 1e-9. Timers that change rates now split
the window, which also fixes today's retroactive re-pricing.

**Tap transport.** `taps {node, n, weak, t0, t1}`, coalesced by the client (1 s or 20 taps) and
predicted locally; a token bucket in the state (15/s, burst 30) clamps silently, so an honest
tapper never sees a rollback. A slim idempotency record with no stored state, kept 1 hour (D59
amended); no `event_log` row per batch; the queue survives a network error. Floaters and
particles are pooled (BitmapText), and the running counter is drawn outside React.

**The meta layer** moves into the base document as `state.meta` (Ash, Ash ever, tree, loadout,
scrap, blueprints, crew levels, logbook, launches), so tree buys and the launch are predicted
(D64). The launch is a pure function, `launch(state, now) → newRun(meta')`, seeded from the
player seed plus the run number. The API overwrites the same row (the version keeps rising)
and logs `launched`; the `legacy` row becomes a server mirror for boards and Exodus; the
`bases` key is unchanged (one perpetual season).

**Content** becomes formulas, not rows: `lines`, `eras`, `kettle`, `tree` (with positions),
`abilities`, `flotsam`, `doctrines`, `milestones`, and effects as data
(`{stat, op: add | inc | more | unlock, value}`) folded by one evaluator. Cross-checks: tree
reachable and acyclic, locale keys present, ring costs rising.

**The simulator** plays a lifetime loop (run, launch rule, carry, next run) over 30 and 90 days
with idler, casual, clicker, optimal and late-joiner archetypes; pacing v2 takes its targets
from 4.5; the RTP tests stay dormant with the casino.

**Reuse** (code surviving with at most light edits):

| Area | Survives | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| domain | ~45% | kept: clock, rng, commands, refusals, world, events, feed, stats, words, recipe graph, missions (~80%), advisor shape, carry plumbing; rewritten: accrual, modifiers, settling, nodes, craft → lines; removed: market, PvP, Signal, upkeep |
| content | ids ~85%, schema ~40% | |
| api | ~80% | kept: auth, command pipeline, push, hub, backups, bot routes; new: taps, launch; removed: cross-base market and PvP |
| web scene | ~75% | kept: camera, layers, terrain, sky, 18×3 building art, nodes, actors, effects; new: Kettle, cinematic, crater, tree view |
| web HUD | ~40% | kept: TopBar, toasts, modals, Cost, AwayModal, SeasonOver; new: Yard, nav, ability rail; Den, Defence, Games, Legacy go |
| discord | ~80% | card content changes |
| sim | ~35% | harness kept; policies and pacing rewritten |

Overall, about 55% of today's code and about 90% of the drawn scene survive. About 85% of
content ids survive, which matters for the icons.

## 11. Risks, failure modes and open decisions

**Failure modes of this philosophy:**

1. **The check-in skeleton leaks through:** 18 materials and slow chains make a muddy clicker.
   Guard: R1 ends with a screenshot review of the first 10 minutes; if they don't read without
   text, fold hide and leather into fat and rope into fibre (15 materials).
2. **Reuse for its own sake:** hour-long trips, meals and crew can feel off-tempo in a
   30-minute run. Guard: trips survive launches, meals are idle buffs, and the simulator
   asserts something to do at every check-in.
3. **Settling correctness:** converters plus auto-buy plus the window is the hardest code here;
   property tests come before content.
4. **Tap load and jank** on the VPS and phones: batching and pooling land in R0 and R1.
5. **Balance blow-ups** (Glow × tree × milestones): one stacking order, a magnitude budget, the
   simulator's bands inside `pnpm test`.
6. **Friends at two speeds:** the 3× band, Ashfall, Newcomer's luck, a generous window.
7. **Wiping a live season** about two weeks old: announce it, give a Founder gift.

**Open decisions for the owner:**

1. Names: Ash, Glow, Haul, the Kettle, the Big Red, Hands, Foremen, Flotsam, Rally, Doctrines,
   Ashfall, Convoys, Pennants, Exodus, Charts.
2. Remove seasons as a wipe (recommended), keeping a monthly leaderboard?
3. The window: 6 h to start, 100% offline efficiency?
4. The casino: park (recommended) or delete?
5. Make the primary button orange so red means only the Big Red?
6. Sound (none exists): procedural blips now, owner sounds later?
7. Raider skiffs in R5, or cut defence for good?
8. Launching from Discord: no (the cinematic is the payoff)?
9. Season 1: end it at R2 with a ceremony (recommended), or wipe now?
